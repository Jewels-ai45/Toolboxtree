Setting a Weekly Priority List That Actually Sticks

Why three priorities beats a ten-item to-do list, and how a simple urgent/important matrix helps.

Most weekly to-do lists fail for the same reason: they're too long. Fifteen items feels productive to write down, but by Wednesday half of them have slid to next week and the list starts to feel like evidence against you rather than a plan. The fix isn't a better app — it's a shorter list.

Start with three, not ten

The "Rule of Three" is simple: instead of a running list of everything you could do, pick three priorities for the week — the things that would make it a genuinely good week if they got done, even if nothing else did. Everything else is just a task, not a priority, and tasks can wait, get delegated, or get dropped without the week feeling like a failure.

Three works because it's small enough to actually hold in your head, but big enough to cover the handful of things that really move the needle. Ten items spreads your attention so thin that nothing gets finished properly; three forces you to decide what actually matters before Monday starts, not while you're already behind on Wednesday.

Separate "urgent" from "important" — they're not the same thing

Once the big three are set, everything else — the smaller tasks — tends to fall into one of four boxes, depending on two separate questions: is it urgent (does it have a deadline pressing on it), and is it important (does it actually matter for your goals)?

Most overloaded to-do lists are overloaded because everything sits in "urgent," whether or not it's actually important. Sorting tasks into this matrix for five minutes at the start of the week does most of the prioritising work for you — the "delete" quadrant alone usually removes a third of the list.

Review midweek, not just on Sunday

A priority list set once on Monday and never looked at again drifts. A two-minute midweek check — are the three priorities still on track, has anything urgent genuinely important come up — catches problems while there's still time to fix them, instead of discovering on Friday that the week went sideways.
